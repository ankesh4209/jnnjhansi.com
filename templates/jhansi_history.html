History of Jhansi City & Fort

The immortal legacy of valour, patriotism, and the 1857 First War of Indian Independence.

Symbol of Courage, Valour & Self-Respect

Jhansi city, situated between the sacred rivers Pahunj and Betwa, is a symbol of bravery, courage, and self-respect. In ancient times, Jhansi was a part of Chedi Rashtra, Jejak Bhukti, Jajhoti, and Bundelkhand. Jhansi was a stronghold of the Chandela kings under the early name Balwant Nagar.

"बुंदेले हरबोलों के मुँह हमने सुनी कहानी थी, खूब लड़ी मर्दानी वह तो झाँसी वाली रानी थी।"

1613: Construction of Jhansi Fort by Raja Bir Singh Deo

In the 17th century, under Raja Bir Singh Deo of Orchha, Jhansi rose to prominence. In 1613, Raja Bir Singh Deo constructed the massive Jhansi Fort on the hilltop known as Bangira. The fort was constructed with 10 imposing entry gates and impregnable granite bastions.

1729 - 1742: Maratha Rule & Naroshanker's Expansion

Maharaja Chhatrasal Bundela defeated Muhammad Khan Bangash with the assistance of Peshwa Baji Rao I. In gratitude, a third of his kingdom, including Jhansi, was offered to the Marathas. In 1742, Naroshanker was appointed governor of Jhansi, who extended the fort (Shankergarh) and constructed Rani Mahal and the MahaLaxmi Temple.

1857: The First War of Indian Independence & Maharani Lakshmibai

Following Maharaja Gangadhar Rao's demise in 1853, the British East India Company applied Lord Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse. In 1857, Rani Lakshmibai took command, boldly declaring "Main Apni Jhansi Nahin Doongi" (I shall not surrender my Jhansi). For two weeks, her forces fought valiantly against British General Hugh Rose, forever cementing Jhansi as the epicenter of Indian freedom struggle.
